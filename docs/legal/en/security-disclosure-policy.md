# Security and Vulnerability Disclosure Policy — NexLink

**Version 0.3.0-draft** · Not yet published · §30

> **Scope.** Applies to all NexLink Products and the servers supporting them.
>
> **Draft.** Not reviewed by a lawyer. See `docs/legal/README.md`.

---

## 1. Scope

This policy covers the NexLink Social Android application, its homeserver at
`nexlink.thvjq.com.au`, the web client, the account deletion page, and the
supporting services operated for it.

It does **not** cover third-party services the Service depends on — Cloudflare,
Google Firebase, LiveKit — which have their own disclosure programmes, nor the
upstream Matrix protocol or the Synapse homeserver, which should be reported to
their maintainers.

---

## 2. The honest constraint

This Service is operated by one individual as a personal project. There is:

- **No bug bounty** and no payment of any kind.
- **No guaranteed response time**, though see §5 for what is intended.
- **No dedicated security team.** The person who reads your report is the person
  who wrote the code and runs the server.

That is stated so nobody wastes effort expecting a corporate process.

---

## 3. How to report

Contact the Operator at the address published on the **Contact Page**, `https://thvjq.com.au/nexlink/contact`,
with:

- What you found, and where.
- The steps to reproduce it.
- What an attacker could do with it.
- Whether you have disclosed it elsewhere.

If the issue is serious, say so in the subject line.

---

## 4. What we ask of you

- **Do not access, modify or delete data belonging to other users.** Use your
  own account, or ask for a test account.
- **Do not degrade the Service** for others — no denial of service, no volume
  testing.
- **Do not use social engineering** against the Operator or any user.
- **Give reasonable time to fix** before disclosing publicly. 90 days is the
  usual expectation; less if the issue is being actively exploited, and the
  Operator will not object to earlier disclosure in that case.

Testing performed within these bounds will not be treated as unauthorised by the
Operator.

---

## 5. What you can expect

| Stage | Intended timeframe |
|---|---|
| Acknowledgement | 7 days |
| Initial assessment | 14 days |
| Fix for a critical issue | As fast as possible; a temporary shutdown is an acceptable mitigation |
| Fix for a lower-severity issue | Best effort |
| Credit | Offered, and declined on request |

These are intentions rather than commitments. A solo operator may be unavailable.

---

## 6. Severity, and what would trigger a shutdown

Some issues are worse than the Service being down. The Operator will take the
Service offline rather than continue running it if:

- Message plaintext is exposed server-side.
- Private keys or the server signing key are exposed.
- An attacker can authenticate as another user.
- An attacker can silently add a device to another user's account.

Availability is not the highest value here. Confidentiality is.

---

## 7. What is not a vulnerability

- That the server holds metadata. This is documented in the Privacy Policy and
  is a property of the design.
- That reactions are sent unencrypted. Documented, and a property of the Matrix
  protocol.
- That a recipient can screenshot a message.
- That there is no uptime guarantee.
- Missing security headers with no demonstrated impact.
- Reports generated by an automated scanner with no verification.

---

## 8. If a breach occurs

The Operator will notify affected users directly and promptly, and will notify
the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, or the Swiss Federal
Data Protection and Information Commissioner, where required.

A notification will state what happened, what data was involved, what has been
done about it, and what the user should do. Because content is end-to-end
encrypted, a server-side breach would expose metadata rather than messages —
and that distinction will be stated accurately rather than used to minimise the
event.
