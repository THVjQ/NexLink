# Legal documents — §4.7

**These are DRAFTS prepared for the operator's review. None of them has been
reviewed by a lawyer, and I am not qualified to give legal advice.**

What they *are*: an accurate description of how this service actually behaves,
written from the measured findings in the specification rather than from a
template. Every factual claim in them traces to something verified against the
running system — which is the part a lawyer cannot do for you and the part that
makes the documents worth anything.

What they still need: a review by someone qualified, particularly on the
Australian Consumer Law guarantees (which **cannot** be excluded by a warranty
disclaimer, however the document is worded) and on whether the Privacy Act 1988
applies to a service of this size.

| Document | Version | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `terms-of-service.md` | 0.1.0-draft | What the service is, acceptable use, no warranty, termination, governing law |
| `privacy-policy.md` | 0.1.0-draft | Every data type, retention, lawful bases, rights, the honest metadata disclosure |
| `content-policy.md` | 0.1.0-draft | What is not permitted — required by Play's UGC policy |
| `transparency-note.md` | 0.1.0-draft | What the operator can and cannot see, in plain language |

§4.7 requires a semantic version on each, recorded against every acceptance
(§9.6.2), so that re-acceptance on material change is mechanical. The versions
above are `0.1.0-draft` and must become `1.0.0` before §33.7's private launch.

**Do not publish these as they stand.** §9.6.1's screen already tells users the
truth; a policy document that contradicts it would be worse than having none.
